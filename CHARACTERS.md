# Learn With Obscure character guide

Use this guide whenever writing dialogue, narration, captions, or voiceover for
the channel. The characters are learning partners, not an expert lecturing to
an audience.

## Obscure — curious learner

Obscure is the chibi learner and the audience's stand-in. He is eager,
good-natured, and a little impulsive rather than unintelligent. He asks the
questions a new learner may be too shy to ask: “why?”, “how?”, “what if?”, and
“can we really do that?” He occasionally reaches a funny, almost-right
conclusion, then understands the idea once it is explained.

- Let Obscure create a real problem before introducing a concept.
- Give him short, natural questions that unlock an important explanation.
- Let him state the concept back in his own words near the end of a section.
- Never make him the joke or call him stupid; his curiosity is useful.

Example voice:

> “Why can we ignore that whole half? Those numbers did nothing wrong.”

## Zeno Sensei — AI robot mentor

Zeno Sensei is a calm, patient AI robot who explains ideas precisely. He has dry,
gentle humor and teases Obscure only affectionately. His jokes should make the
concept easier to remember, never obscure the answer. He corrects Obscure
without talking down to him.

- Answer the question first, then add a brief playful line when it fits.
- Prefer clear analogies, visible evidence, and short explanations.
- Be lightly deadpan: “Efficient, not ruthless. Mostly.”
- Do not make Zeno Sensei smug, mean, or needlessly sarcastic.

Example voice:

> “Because the list is sorted, everything left of eight is even smaller. They
> are cleared of suspicion—by mathematics, not vibes.”

## Their teaching rhythm

1. Obscure arrives with a concrete task or confusion.
2. Zeno Sensei identifies the key prerequisite or idea.
3. Obscure asks a genuine “why” or “what happens if” question.
4. Zeno Sensei answers while the animation makes the answer visible.
5. Obscure summarizes the insight in plain language.

Keep dialogue captions short enough to read on a mobile screen. Put a speaker
name in on-screen captions (`Obscure:` or `Zeno Sensei:`). When voiceover is added,
use the full dialogue in the scene's `speak()` call and align its audio duration
to the visual beat.
